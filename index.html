01 · Ten Minutes from Dotonbori
A short ride · a different world

Just south of Dotonbori,
a hub most people avoid.

Just ten minutes south of Dotonbori is Shin-Imamiya Station in Nishinari Ward.

It's a major transport hub — surrounded by some of the cheapest, and even free, hotels in Japan.

And yet, most people avoid it. To understand why, we first have to look at where it is — and what sits right next to one of Osaka's brightest tourist streets.

02 · The Reputation, and What It Hides
Cheap rooms · a heavy name

The most dangerous name in Osaka.

Nishinari is known as the most dangerous neighborhood in Osaka. It's tied to dirty streets and the Tobita Shinchi red-light district, and the name itself carries a sense of shame in Japan.

But behind that reputation is a story most people have never heard. In the tiny rooms of these cheap hotels, some residents die alone — with no one noticing.

This neighborhood tells us something important about Japan's aging crisis — and why it matters to all of us.

03 · A Generation Left Behind
The world's most aged society

They built modern Japan.
Now they grow old alone.

Japan is the world's most aged society — over 29% of its people are 65 or older. After WWII, men came from all over Japan to Nishinari for work in construction, at the ports, and in factories. They built the roads and bridges of Japan's biggest economic boom.

But they were day laborers — no stable contracts, no pension, no family to return to. Now those same men are in their 70s and 80s, with no one to check if they're still alive.

04 · So What Is Being Done?
Local response · regional reality

Small acts against a quiet crisis.

  • Government officers visit elderly residents to check whether they are still alive and well.
  • Community groups like Cocoroom run cafés where older men can come in and talk to someone.
  • Low rents and doya hotels are kept open to house these men cheaply and stably.

And this is not only Japan's problem. Across Asia — in Japan, Taiwan, and South Korea — millions gave their youth to build modern cities and economies. As they grow old, many face the same quiet ending as the men of Nishinari: no family nearby, dying alone.
